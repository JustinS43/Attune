Assembly, step by step

About an hour with two people at the table. Do the steps in order and don't plug the USB cable into the laptop until step 12. Numbers in brackets are the parts list.

On the table first: sunglasses, C922 webcam, UNO R3, Grove Base Shield, 2 sound sensors, touch sensor, 2 LED sockets, SG92R servo with its horns, mini breadboard, 5 Grove cables, 5 male to male jumpers, USB A-to-B cable, tape, a marker, and the laptop. Leave the DC motor, the 0.1 µF capacitor and the motor driver board in the box.

Build

  1. Label every cable, both ends. Wrap a masking-tape flag near each end and write on it: L SOUND A0 R SOUND A1 TOUCH D2 L LED D5 R LED D6 on the Grove cables, and SERVO D9 5V GND on the jumpers. Swapped left and right is the most likely wiring mistake.
  2. Stack the Base Shield on the UNO. Line up every pin, check none is bent outside a socket, then press both ends down evenly. If the shield has a 3.3 V / 5 V switch, set it to 5 V.
  3. Webcam [1]. Clip it to the centre of the bridge, then wrap two strips of tape around the clip and the frame. You'll aim it in step 19.
  4. Sound sensors [2]. Tape one to the outside of each arm, just behind the hinge, microphone facing outward. Tape above and below the microphone, never over it. Plug in L SOUND A0 on the left and R SOUND A1 on the right.
  5. LED sockets [3]. Tape one at the outer front corner of each lens and bend the LED so it points back at your eye. L LED D5 on the left, R LED D6 on the right.
  6. Touch sensor [4]. Tape it to the right arm, behind the right sound sensor, where your finger lands naturally. One thin layer of tape over the pad is fine. Plug in TOUCH D2.
  7. Servo tapper [5]. Push the single-arm horn on loosely (you'll set it in step 18) and fold a small bump of tape onto its tip. Tape the servo body to the outside of the right temple tip, shaft pointing down, so the horn swings under the arm and the bump reaches the skin behind your ear.
  8. Route the cables. Run every cable along the arms with a strip of tape every few centimetres, then let them drop behind your ears to the chest. Tape the servo lead with the right-side cables.
  9. Grove cables into the shield [7, 9]. Plug each labelled cable into the shield port with the same name: A0, A1, D2, D5, D6. The plugs only fit one way round.
  10. Breadboard and servo [8, 10]. Jumper the shield's 5V pin to the breadboard + rail (red) and a GND pin to the − rail (black). The servo's plug has three holes, so it doesn't go into the breadboard: push one male to male jumper into each hole instead. Run the jumper from the brown hole to −, the one from red to +, and the one from orange to the shield's D9 header socket; that's the SERVO D9 jumper. Wrap tape around the plug so the jumpers can't pull out. Keep the optional capacitor [15] ready for step 17.
  11. Check before power. Read every label against the pin map, and make sure no bare jumper end touches another. Then tape the UNO, shield and breadboard together and tape the pack to your shirt at chest height, leaving slack in the cables so a head turn can't pull them out.

First power-up, in this order

  1. USB only. Plug the UNO into the laptop with the USB A-to-B cable, webcam still unplugged. The ON LED lights and the "L" LED (pin 13) shows the firmware status. Nothing lit, or the laptop warns about the USB port: unplug at once and look for a 5V-to-GND short on the breadboard.
  2. Firmware. If the board is new, upload firmware/attune_rig as described in firmware/README.md. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200: a READY line, then a stream of level lines. Close the Serial Monitor before starting the engine.
  3. Sound. Start the engine (docs/hardware/wiring.md has the commands) and clap beside each ear in turn. The left level must jump for a left clap. If it's the other way round, swap the A0 and A1 plugs.
  4. Touch. Tap, hold, double tap and triple tap the pad; each gesture should show up in the console.
  5. LEDs. Send the left test pattern, then the right. Each must light on its own side, visible without dazzling you.
  6. Servo, last. Send a test pattern and watch the horn move and return. If the board resets or the USB link drops when it moves, put the 100–470 µF capacitor across the rails (stripe to −) and try again.
  7. Set the tap. With the servo at rest, pull the horn off and push it back on at the angle where the bump just clears your head, so each move gives a light tap. Tighten the horn screw.
  8. Webcam. Plug it into the laptop, open the lens view in Chrome and aim it straight ahead. Then do the bench tests in docs/hardware/wiring.md and log the results there.

Notes

Things that will bite on demo day.

  • It's front-heavy, mostly from the webcam. The servo at the back helps a little; take the rig off between demos.
  • Everything runs from the two USB cables, so stay within reach of the laptop. The servo draws its current from the UNO's USB 5 V; keep taps short and don't let the horn jam against your head.
  • If a Grove cable doesn't reach your chest, push male to male jumpers into its plug to extend it and tape the joint so it can't pull apart.
  • The UNO R3 has no LED matrix. Its status shows only on the small "L" LED beside pin 13, under the shield's edge.
  • Only enroll teammates who agree to it. Strangers stay session-only.
  • Sizes in the model are approximate.
Never set off a real alarm at the venue. Play doorbell and smoke-alarm recordings from the JBL Go instead. The alerts are a demo, not a smoke alarm.

Gestures and signals

What the wearer does and feels.

  • Tap the touch sensor = yes. Hold = no.
  • Double tap = save this person (they are asked to consent first).
  • Triple tap = pause.
  • An alarm flashes the LED on the side it came from, and the servo taps with it.

In the kit but not used: the DC motor, the 0.1 µF motor capacitor and the Grove I²C Motor Driver (L298 V1.3). The servo on D9 does the buzzing, so there is no motor driver on I²C.
